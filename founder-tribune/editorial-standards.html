1. Our purpose and independence

Founder Tribune reports on the people who start and run companies, and on the news that affects them. Our first duty is to our readers. We aim to be accurate, fair and useful.

Our journalists decide what we cover and how. No advertiser, sponsor, investor, source or owner may direct, approve or veto our news coverage. Founder Tribune is published by [Company legal name]. We disclose in a story any ownership or funding relationship that a reasonable reader would want to know about.

2. Accuracy and verification

  • We check facts before we publish. Names, titles, figures, dates and quotes are confirmed against a primary source wherever one exists.
  • We tell readers how we know something: who said it, which document shows it, or what we saw.
  • Financial figures such as funding rounds, revenue and valuations are attributed. We say when a figure comes from the company alone and could not be confirmed independently.
  • We distinguish clearly between fact, analysis and opinion.
  • A second editor reads every story before publication. Stories that make serious allegations receive legal review.
  • Headlines, summaries and social posts must be supported by the story beneath them.

3. Sources and attribution

We name our sources whenever we can. A named source can be judged by readers; an unnamed one asks them to trust us.

We grant anonymity only when the information is important, cannot be obtained on the record, and the source faces a real risk such as losing a job. An editor must know the source's identity and approve the decision. We tell readers why the source is not named and what their position is in general terms. We do not let anonymous sources make personal attacks.

We credit other publications when we rely on their reporting, and link to it. We do not plagiarise. Quotes are the words the person used; we do not alter them beyond removing verbal hesitations, and we mark any cut.

"On the record", "on background" and "off the record" are agreed with a source before they speak, not afterwards.

4. Fairness and the right to respond

People and companies we write about critically are given a fair opportunity to respond before publication, with enough detail and time to do so. We include their response, or say that they declined or did not reply.

We do not show stories to sources before publication or give them approval over what we write. We may read back a quote or check a technical point for accuracy.

We take care when reporting on people who are not public figures, on children, and on grief, health or personal finances. We weigh a person's privacy against the public interest in the story.

5. Corrections and updates

When we get something wrong, we fix it promptly and say so. A note on the story explains what was changed and when. We do not quietly edit significant errors. Our full corrections policy explains how to report an error.

We rarely remove published stories. If a story is seriously flawed we retract it with an explanation and leave that notice in place.

6. Conflicts of interest

  • Journalists do not report on companies in which they, or their immediate family, hold shares or another financial interest, and they declare such interests to their editor.
  • Staff do not trade on information gained through their work before it is published.
  • We do not accept payment, gifts, travel or hospitality from the people and companies we cover, other than items of trivial value. Where we accept access we could not otherwise get, such as a press trip, we say so in the story.
  • Staff do not do paid work for companies they might cover, and do not take part in political campaigning in ways that could call our independence into question.
  • Personal relationships with a source or subject are declared to an editor, who decides whether someone else should handle the story.

7. Advertising, sponsorship and paid content

Advertising pays for journalism, and it stays separate from it. Coverage cannot be bought. We do not charge for news stories, interviews or mentions, and a pitch is judged only on its news value.

Content paid for by a sponsor is clearly labelled "Sponsored" and is produced by a team separate from the newsroom. Newsroom journalists do not write sponsored content. Affiliate links, where used, are disclosed in the story.

8. News and opinion

Opinion articles are labelled as opinion and carry the writer's name. They reflect the writer's view, and must still be accurate on the facts. We aim to publish a range of perspectives and tell readers about any relevant interest the writer has in the subject.

9. How we use artificial intelligence

We use AI tools to help with research, transcription, summarising documents and, for some news desk stories, preparing a first draft from published sources. People remain responsible for everything we publish.

  • An editor checks AI-assisted stories against their sources. For most stories this happens before publication.
  • Our automated news desk follows what is trending and may publish a story before an editor has read it, but only when at least two independent news outlets confirm it. Until an editor has reviewed such a story, a note at the end says it was published automatically.
  • Social media posts are used only as leads. We do not report something because it is trending; we report it when reliable sources confirm it.
  • Stories drafted with AI assistance say so, list their sources and carry the "Tribune News Desk" byline. We do not put a reporter's name on work they did not report.
  • We do not use AI to invent quotes, people, events or images presented as real.
  • We do not publish AI-generated photographs as news images. Illustrations made with AI are labelled.
  • We do not put confidential source material into AI tools that could expose it.

10. Photographs and graphics

News photographs are not altered beyond routine adjustments such as cropping and exposure. Stock or library photographs used to illustrate a story are credited and are not presented as showing the specific people or events in it. Charts show data fairly, with sources named.

11. How our journalists work

Our journalists identify themselves and their publication when gathering news. They do not misrepresent themselves or record people secretly, except in rare cases approved by the editor in chief where the public interest is strong and there is no other way to get the information. They treat the people they deal with courteously, and they apply these standards to what they post on social media.

12. Questions and complaints

If you believe we have fallen short of these standards, write to corrections@foundertribune.com or use the form on our corrections page. A senior editor who did not work on the story will review your complaint and reply within ten working days. If you are not satisfied, you can ask for a review by the editor in chief, whose decision we will explain in writing.
